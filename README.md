# The Universe
## What Is the Universe?
The universe is everything that exists: space, time, matter, energy, and the physical laws that govern them. It includes galaxies, stars, planets, black holes, radiation, dark matter, and dark energy. The observable universe is about __93 billion__ light-years in diameter, but the total universe may be far larger—or even infinite. Scientists study the universe using astronomy, physics, cosmology, and _mathematics_.



## The Beginning: The Big Bang

1. The leading theory for the origin of the universe is the Big Bang.
2.  Around ***13.8 billion years*** ago, the universe began as an extremely hot, dense point and expanded rapidly. 
3. This expansion continues today. 
4. The Big Bang was not an explosion in space; it was an expansion of space itself. 
5. In the first seconds, fundamental particles formed. 
6. Within minutes, simple elements like hydrogen and helium were created. 
7. Hundreds of millions of years later, the first stars ignited.



## The Structure of the Universe

* The universe is not randomly distributed. 
* Matter forms a large-scale structure often described as a cosmic web. 
  * Galaxies cluster together in groups and superclusters, separated by vast empty regions called voids. 
  * Gravity is the main force shaping these structures. 
    * The distribution of galaxies provides evidence for dark matter, which influences structure formation through gravitational effects.


## Galaxies

Galaxies are massive systems containing stars, gas, dust, and dark matter. There are hundreds of billions of galaxies in the observable universe. They come in different shapes: spiral, elliptical, and irregular. Our galaxy, the Milky Way, is a barred spiral galaxy containing over 100 billion stars. At its center lies a supermassive black hole.

## Black Holes

Black holes are regions of space where gravity is so strong that not even light can escape. They form when massive stars collapse at the end of their life cycles. At the center of most galaxies, including the Milky Way, lies a supermassive black hole. Black holes warp spacetime and can merge, producing gravitational waves detected on Earth.
